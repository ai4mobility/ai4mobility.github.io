## Course purpose

This course introduces emerging and pragmatic AI technologies for transportation mobility
systems. It focuses on AI methods already being implemented in transportation
infrastructure, vehicle systems, mobility services, and agency workflows, as well as AI
technologies actively under development in research and industry.

The goal is to understand how these technologies are built, what is actually happening
behind the "black box," and how they can be applied responsibly to real transportation
problems.

The course serves two tracks at once:

- **Research-oriented students** build the ability to understand AI models, read emerging
  AI papers critically, and adapt or develop AI methods for transportation research.
- **Practice-oriented students** build practical skills for using AI to improve
  productivity, support data analysis, automate routine tasks, and integrate AI tools into
  transportation engineering and agency workflows.

## Student learning outcomes

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

1. Describe core AI concepts and methods relevant to transportation and mobility systems,
   including neural networks, representation learning, transformers, generative models,
   reinforcement learning, and agentic AI.
2. Apply AI tools, interactive notebook-based exercises, and transportation datasets to
   complete structured mobility analysis or modeling tasks.
3. Interpret outputs from selected AI models and assess their relevance, limitations, and
   potential sources of error in transportation applications.
4. Review AI-related mobility research or technical materials and summarize the problem
   setting, method, data, findings, and limitations.
5. Develop a group project that applies an AI method, AI-assisted workflow, or
   hardware-integrated AI prototype to a transportation or mobility problem.
6. Communicate project methods, results, limitations, and practical implications in written
   and oral form.
7. Discuss responsible use considerations for human-in-the-loop AI in transportation,
   including reliability, robustness, safety, privacy, interpretability, human oversight,
   and professional accountability.

## Course format

Lectures, guided discussions, demonstrations, hands-on AI labs, and experimental
implementation activities. Fourteen class meetings of roughly 2.5 hours each. Many sessions
include interactive notebook-based labs that run in Google Colab — no local setup required.

The course also incorporates hardware demonstrations and experimental platforms, including
AI edge devices, self-driving development devices (comma 3x), edge AI chips (NVIDIA Jetson
Nano), AI desktop robots (M5Stack), and related mobility sensing or computing systems.
These activities show how AI mobility technologies move from algorithms to real-world
implementation on vehicles, roadside sensors, embedded computing devices, and robotic
platforms.

All course hardware is instructor-provided and used under instructor supervision. No student
is required to supply a vehicle or personal equipment.

## The group project

The final project is completed in teams. Both research-oriented and practice-oriented
topics are offered. Students are encouraged to use AI methods, hardware-integrated
prototypes, or agentic AI workflows to address real transportation problems.

Project teams receive feedback from an **advisory committee of local transportation
experts**, connecting technical work to practical transportation needs and professional
expectations. Because project materials are shared with reviewers outside the university,
students may request that identifying information be withheld, and no student is required to
share proprietary or personal data.

Projects that involve collecting video, imagery, or other identifiable data about people
require instructor approval and IRB consultation **before** any data collection begins.

Group project grades may include both a team component and an individual component. Peer
evaluation may be used to assess individual contribution, collaboration, and reliability.

## Course policies

**Late work.** Assignments are submitted through Canvas by the posted deadline. Late
submissions may receive a grade penalty unless prior approval has been granted or the delay
is due to a documented emergency. Because many labs and project activities build on previous
work, contact the instructor as early as possible if you anticipate difficulty. Late work may
not be accepted after solutions or feedback have been released.

**Make-up work.** Permitted for documented illness, emergency, religious observance,
university-approved activity, or other circumstances approved by the instructor. Notify the
instructor in advance where possible.

**AI tools.** Because this course is about AI, students are encouraged to use AI tools to
support learning, coding, debugging, writing, brainstorming, data analysis, and project
development. Students remain responsible for the accuracy, originality, documentation, and
explanation of all submitted work. AI-generated output should be critically reviewed and
verified. Automated AI-similarity indicators are not treated, on their own, as evidence of
misconduct in this course.

**Hardware.** Students must follow all instructor-provided safety, privacy, data-use, and
equipment-handling instructions. Course hardware, datasets, and experimental platforms may
not be operated, modified, removed, or used outside approved course activities without
permission.

**First-day attendance.** USF requires completion of a first-day attendance activity.
Students who do not complete it may be reported as not attending and dropped from the course.

**Academic integrity.** Enrolled students follow the
[USF Academic Integrity policy](https://www.usf.edu/provost/students/academic-integrity.aspx).

**Accommodations.** Students requiring accommodations should contact
[Student Accessibility Services](https://www.usf.edu/student-affairs/student-accessibility/)
early in the semester.

## For self-learners

The lecture notes, labs, and readings on this site are openly licensed and free to work
through outside the USF course. If you're following along independently, the schedule above
is a reasonable pace, though most self-learners take longer than a semester. Labs run in
Google Colab with one click. Grades, Canvas access, hardware sessions, and the advisory
committee review are limited to enrolled students.
