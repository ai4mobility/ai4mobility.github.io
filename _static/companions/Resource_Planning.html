Before the storm: what should be ready?

How dark signal-hours change with the number of generators, beacon kits, trucks, fuel trucks and built-in beacons, over simulated storms on the Tampa network. Example numbers, not FDOT figures.

What the runs say

    1. Generators and beacon kits

    Each line is a number of beacon kits. Each point is the average of simulated storms of that level. Each panel has its own vertical scale. Six generator trucks; fuel trucks are sized to the generator count, so fuel is not the limit here. Extra delay is what a 4-way stop costs against the working signal, summed over the signals without signal control (see Dispatch_Choices.html).

    2. What it takes to meet a target

    The smallest plan on the grid above that meets each target, counting generators first and then kits.

    3. Fuel trucks

    Generators need fuel to stay on. The six-hour rule refuels the emptiest tank once it is half empty. The look-ahead rule starts earlier when trucks would fall behind and keeps Priority 1 generators running first. The 40-generator plans have 80 beacon kits and 6 generator trucks; the 80-generator plans have 160 kits and 10 generator trucks.

    4. Generator trucks

    More trucks place the same equipment sooner on day 1.

    5. Built-in beacons

    A built-in power-outage beacon makes a signal flash by itself when power is lost. Here they go to the highest-priority signals first.

    6. Where to stage

    The same trucks and equipment, staged at one yard or split between the two assumed yards. Average risk score.

    7. Cover every dark signal first, or place generators first?

    A generator takes about three times as long to place as a beacon. With 80 generators, 160 beacon kits and 6 generator trucks, the trucks cannot place every generator and still reach every other dark signal before the first night. This report holds generators back so that every dark signal gets at least a beacon. The other choice places generators where they remove the most risk and lets some low-priority signals stay dark for a night.
