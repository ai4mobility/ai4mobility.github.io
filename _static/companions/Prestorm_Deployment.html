Before landfall: zones, teams and the Priority 1 rule

What the SOP asks FDOT to decide three hours before the storm, tested on simulated storms on the Tampa network. The zones are assumed. Example numbers, not FDOT figures.

What the runs say

    1. The zones

    A zone is a few corridors, as on the District 7 zone maps. The real Hillsborough zones are not in our copy of the SOP, so these five are assumed: signals are grouped into compact areas, and each zone keeps whole stretches of a corridor. Shapes show priority, as on the District 7 maps.

    2. Who covers which zone

    Three hours before the storm, FDOT gives each generator team its zone. The rows compare ways to do that with the same teams and equipment (40 generators, 80 beacon kits). The first row is not an SOP option: one dispatcher who sees everything and can send any truck anywhere. It shows the best the same resources can do. Each row is the average of simulated storms.

    3. Priority 1 first, or all dark signals?

    The SOP has the TMC decide whether generator teams "deploy only at Priority #1 intersections or to all non-operational signals within their assigned zones." Here "Priority 1 first" means: no generator goes to a Priority 2 or 3 signal while any Priority 1 signal can still take one; after that, teams serve every signal. Beacons go to any dark signal under both rules. Teams are assigned by forecast work and may move when they have nothing left to do.
