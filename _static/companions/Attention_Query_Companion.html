The weights are not stored anywhere. They are computed, fresh, every time the model reads a sentence — which is why they can depend on the sentence. Here is the whole calculation for the query word in this head.

In words: the query word writes down what it is looking for (q), every other word advertises what it has (k), the dot product scores the match, softmax turns the scores into a set of weights that add to 1, and the output is that weighted average of what each word actually carries (v).

Going deeper — why divide by 8?

Each head here works in 64 dimensions, so √d = 8. A dot product of two 64-dimensional vectors grows with the dimension; feed raw dot products of 30 to 40 into a softmax and it saturates, one weight goes to 1 and the rest to 0, and the gradient through it dies. Dividing by √d keeps the scores in a range where softmax stays soft and trainable. It is the same reason you standardise a covariate before a logit model rather than feeding it in raw units. (Vaswani et al., Attention is all you need, NeurIPS 2017, §3.2.1.)

Off words

Nothing in the arithmetic on the previous tab mentions language. A token is whatever you decide it is, and the query/key/value machinery works the same way:

If a token is…then attention is learning…
a detector station, one 5-minute binwhich upstream stations this one's speed depends on right now — an adjacency matrix that differs at 8am and 5pm
a vehicle in the ego vehicle's neighbourhoodwhich surrounding vehicle the driver is actually responding to — the multi-anticipative term a car-following model puts in by hand
a signal phase in a cyclewhich earlier phase is setting up the queue this one has to serve
a 16×16 patch of a dashcam framewhich parts of the scene the label for this patch depends on

The declared, fixed, symmetric adjacency matrix you would write down for a CTM or a spatial model becomes a learned, input-dependent one. That is the whole trade: you stop telling the model who the neighbours are, and you pay for it in data.

Fit card — self-attention

Idea in one sentenceEach token builds its own summary of the sequence by taking a weighted average of every token, with weights it computes from its own content.
Why it works (the logic)The weights are a function of the input, so the same token gets a different summary in a different context — and the "who is related to whom" structure is learned rather than declared.
Fits these tasksSequence and set problems where the right neighbourhood changes with conditions: corridor speed prediction, trajectory prediction, incident narrative mining, scene understanding. (extraction · classification · prediction)
Needs this dataLong sequences, many of them. Attention has no built-in notion of locality, so everything a convolution or a CTM gets for free must be learned from examples — or supplied through the position encoding.
Breaks whenCost is quadratic in sequence length. Data is thin. The neighbourhood you care about really is fixed and known — then you are paying to learn something you could have written down. And attention maps invite over-interpretation: they are not explanations.
Keep the classical method whenThe physical adjacency is the right one and stable, the sequence is short, or you need a model whose behaviour you can argue for in a hearing. A declared graph plus a linear model is often within noise of a transformer on corridor speed — and it is auditable.
